# Gotchas (learned from real applications)

Skim these before the gap analysis and again before preflight.

## Data and documents
- **Spelling mismatches across documents** (e.g. a tenant's name misspelt on a tenancy). Don't demand a reissue; explain it in the cover letter, ask the issuer to fix their records, and use the passport spelling everywhere you control.
- **File extensions lie.** A ".pdf" may be a JPEG. Check with `file` and convert before uploading.
- **Bank statements reprint the current address** on old periods; payslips may still show the old address after a move. Both are fine if the cover letter explains it.
- **An old employer's contract is not evidence for the current job.** Check whose document it is.
- **Emails are weaker than signed letters on headed paper.** They are often still acceptable as supporting evidence. Save them as PDF (print to PDF) for upload.
- **Previous application answers** (e.g. a parent's DOB recorded as 1 January because only the year is known) should stay consistent unless they were wrong.

## Rules and timing
- **Stale figures.** Thresholds, fees, IHS and English levels change. Re-fetch them (SKILL.md, freshness rule 1). Transitional rules may keep an older, lower threshold for people already on the route; ask, don't assume.
- **Stale documents.** Payslip and statement windows roll forward monthly. Give a concrete "submit by" date and say what to re-download after it.
- **"Must wait until N days before expiry"** is a common myth. Check the current guidance on when to apply.
- **Salary changes within the 6-month window**: the employer letter states the current salary and when it started; payslips show the rest. The previous salary is usually not required in the letter.

## Accommodation
- Count rooms only if they're bedrooms or living rooms normally used as such. Conservatories, cold rooms and garages are doubtful. Leave them out if the house passes without them.
- Children under 1 count as 0 persons; ages 1–9 count as 0.5.

## Public funds (high risk)
- **Child Benefit registered in the applicant's name**, often to get National Insurance credits while payments are opted out, can be treated as claiming a public fund by someone with no recourse to public funds. Don't brush it off as "technical".
  - Typical mitigation: the person with recourse (e.g. a British/settled parent) holds the claim instead; the applicant's claim is ended in writing; NI credits are dealt with separately.
  - Disclose it fully in the cover letter and on the form. Concealment can lead to refusal on deception grounds, which is far worse.
  - Get OISC/solicitor advice before submitting. Answer "received" questions literally (if nothing was paid, "No"); "applied/claimed" or vague wording → "Yes" with explanation.
- Exceptions that once existed (e.g. for spouses of certain nationals) may have been removed. Verify the current list rather than relying on old forum posts.

## Process
- Never pay or submit without an explicit human "submit". Never start a second application after a timeout.
- Don't move personal details into reusable templates or external tools.
- When unsure whether something needs legal judgement: it does. Flag it and suggest an OISC-registered adviser.
